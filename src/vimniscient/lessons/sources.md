# Sources for the facts in the lesson texts

The lesson texts are mnemonic: they describe the key being practised (see
`../mnemonics.yaml`), and the rest is about the science of learning and memory.
Every factual claim in them should trace back to one of these.

- **Atkinson & Raugh (1975).** An application of the mnemonic keyword method to
  the acquisition of a Russian vocabulary. *JEP: Human Learning and Memory*
  1(2). The keyword method: link a foreign word to a sound-alike word and an
  image.
- **Bjork (1994).** Memory and metamemory considerations in the training of
  human beings. In *Metacognition*. "Desirable difficulties": conditions that
  make learning feel harder (spacing, testing, interleaving) often make it last.
- **Cowan (2001).** The magical number 4 in short-term memory. *Behavioral and
  Brain Sciences* 24(1). About four chunks.
- **Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (2013).** Improving students'
  learning with effective learning techniques. *Psychological Science in the
  Public Interest* 14(1). High utility: practice testing, distributed practice.
  Moderate: interleaved practice, elaborative interrogation, self-explanation.
  Low: highlighting, rereading, summarization, keyword mnemonic, imagery for
  text.
- **Ebbinghaus (1885).** *Über das Gedächtnis.* Nonsense syllables, tested on
  himself; the forgetting curve, steepest right after learning.
- **Fitts & Posner (1967).** *Human Performance.* Three stages of skill
  learning: cognitive, associative, autonomous.
- **Miller (1956).** The magical number seven, plus or minus two. *Psychological
  Review* 63(2).
- **Roediger & Karpicke (2006).** Test-enhanced learning. *Psychological
  Science* 17(3). Retrieval practice beat restudying on a test a week later,
  though restudying looked better after five minutes.
- **Spacing effect.** Among the best-replicated findings in memory research;
  see Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer (2006), Distributed practice in
  verbal recall tasks, *Psychological Bulletin* 132(3).
- **Sleep and consolidation.** Sleep after learning helps memories stabilise;
  see Diekelmann & Born (2010), The memory function of sleep, *Nature Reviews
  Neuroscience* 11(2).
- **Fluency illusion.** Rereading makes text feel familiar, which people
  mistake for knowing it; see Bjork, Dunlosky & Kornell (2013), Self-regulated
  learning, *Annual Review of Psychology* 64.
- **ed's `t`.** The ed manual: "(.,.)t(.) Copies (i.e., transfers) the
  addressed lines". **grep** takes its name from ed's `g/re/p`.
